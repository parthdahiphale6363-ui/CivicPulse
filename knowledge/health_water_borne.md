# Water-Borne Disease Prevention and Contaminated Supply Response

## Overview
Water-borne diseases—including Acute Diarrheal Disease (ADD), Gastroenteritis, Typhoid, Cholera, and Hepatitis A/E—arise primarily from the ingestion of water contaminated with pathogenic microorganisms. In Pune, the prevention of water-borne epidemics requires coordinated surveillance between the PMC Water Supply Department (monitoring water potability and chlorine residuals) and the PMC Health Department (monitoring clinical disease clusters).

## Contamination Vectors in Municipal Networks
1. **Pipeline Cross-Contamination**: Intermittent water supply schedules cause negative pressure (suction) inside pipelines during supply shutoff. Ingress of sewage from adjacent clogged drains into cracked water pipes results in foul-smelling or muddy tap water upon supply restoration.
2. **Damaged Water Meters and Service Saddles**: Leaks at service connection saddles located inside roadside gutters.
3. **Contaminated Overhead or Underground Tanks**: Silt accumulation, unsealed manhole lids, or backflow in private domestic storage reservoirs.

## Municipal Surveillance and Emergency Protocol
- **Chlorine Residual Monitoring**: Field testing of Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) across tail-end municipal taps. VERIFY: mandatory minimum residual chlorine parts per million (ppm) standard.
- **Super-Chlorination**: Emergency dosing of sodium hypochlorite or bleaching powder into contaminated balancing reservoirs or distribution sectors.
- **Water Sampling & Laboratory Testing**: Municipal health inspectors collect water samples for biological (coliform count) and chemical testing at PMC water testing laboratories.
- **Boiling Water Advisory**: Precautionary municipal advisory urging residents to boil drinking water for at least 10 minutes or use certified filtration during suspected outbreaks.

## Reporting Contaminated Water
- **PMC CARE Helpline**: 1800 1030 222 or WhatsApp 9689900002.
- **Response Turnaround**: VERIFY: emergency water pipeline inspection and sampling SLA.

## Suggested Triage Criteria
Suggested triage criteria, not an official standard:
- **P1 (Critical / Emergency)**: Clustered outbreak of acute gastroenteritis, cholera, or jaundice cases across a neighborhood; tap water issuing with strong sewage odor or visible fecal matter.
- **P2 (High Priority)**: Brown or turbid tap water observed simultaneously across multiple adjacent residential housing societies.
- **P3 (Medium Priority)**: Temporary slight turbidity or chlorine smell following planned municipal maintenance.
- **P4 (Low Priority)**: Routine citizen request for municipal drinking water potability testing certificate.
