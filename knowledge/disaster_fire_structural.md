# Disaster Preparedness and Response: Structural and Electrical Fires

## Overview
Structural and urban fires in Pune pose acute hazards due to the juxtaposition of high-density historic core areas (the traditional Peths) featuring narrow lanes and timber-framed structures, alongside modern multi-story commercial IT parks and high-rise residential towers. Fire safety regulations and emergency firefighting operations fall under the Maharashtra Fire Prevention and Life Safety Measures Act, 2006, executed by the Pune Municipal Corporation Fire Brigade under National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) safety guidelines.

## Primary Fire Risk Scenarios
1. **Electrical Short Circuits**: Overloaded distribution boxes, sub-standard wiring, uninspected meter cabins, and transformer sparks inside residential and commercial basements.
2. **Narrow Peth Lane Congestion**: Encroachments, overhead hanging wires, and parked two-wheelers impeding access for standard hydraulic fire fighting tenders.
3. **High-Rise Staircase Obstructions**: Locked emergency exit doors, blocked fire escapes used as storage areas, and non-functional dry/wet riser piping systems.
4. **Commercial Kitchen and Cylinder Fires**: Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) manifold leaks in unventilated commercial restaurants and bakeries.

## NDMA Fire Safety Guidelines
Based on NDMA guidelines:
1. **Evacuation Protocols**:
   - In case of fire, alert everyone in the building and evacuate immediately using designated staircases. Never use elevators during a fire.
   - If trapped by smoke, crawl low along the floor where the air is cleaner and cooler; cover nose and mouth with a wet cloth.
   - Test door handles with the back of the hand before opening; do not open hot doors.
2. **Prevention and Maintenance**:
   - Conduct regular fire audits, keep fire extinguishers inspected and pressurized, and ensure smoke detectors and automatic sprinkler systems are fully operational.
   - Keep electrical rooms ventilated and free from combustible materials.
3. **If Clothes Catch Fire**:
   - Follow the "STOP, DROP, and ROLL" protocol immediately until flames are extinguished.

## Which Systems Fail First
General planning knowledge (civic systems failure hierarchy during structural fires):
1. **Immediate Failure (0-5 minutes)**: Building internal electrical mains trip; localized elevator power cuts trap occupants if backup mechanisms fail; smoke logging in central stairwells blinds evacuation paths.
2. **Secondary Failure (5-15 minutes)**: Combustible exterior facade panels or false ceilings collapse; internal wet-riser booster water tanks exhaust if not replenished.
3. **Tertiary Failure (15-60 minutes)**: Structural spalling and thermal distortion of unshielded steel/RCC framework leading to partial floor collapse.

## Suggested Triage Criteria
Suggested triage criteria, not an official standard:
- **P1 (Critical / Emergency)**: Active flame or dense toxic smoke in an occupied high-rise building, hospital, or commercial complex with occupants trapped inside.
- **P2 (High Priority)**: Transformer fire or electrical spark explosion threatening adjacent crowded tenements or fuel stations.
- **P3 (Medium Priority)**: Sub-station smoke or electrical sparking without open flame, contained within an isolated electrical cabinet.
- **P4 (Low Priority)**: Routine citizen inspection request for building fire NOC verification or expired fire extinguisher refill audit.
