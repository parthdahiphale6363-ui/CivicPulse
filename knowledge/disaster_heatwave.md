# Disaster Preparedness and Response: Heatwave Management

## Overview
Heatwaves are periods of abnormally high temperatures observed across Maharashtra during pre-monsoon summer months (March through June). The National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) outlines comprehensive mitigation guidelines emphasizing early warning alerts, occupational safety, urban heat island abatement, and emergency medical triage.

## Vulnerable Populations
1. **Outdoor Workers**: Construction laborers, street vendors, delivery personnel, traffic police, and sanitation workers subjected to prolonged direct solar radiation.
2. **High-Risk Medical Groups**: Elderly individuals, infants, pregnant women, and patients with pre-existing cardiovascular or chronic kidney disease.
3. **Dense Urban Slums**: High-density settlements with tin/asbestos roofing lacking adequate cross-ventilation or tree cover.

## NDMA Heatwave Precautions and Do's & Don'ts
Based on NDMA advisory guidelines:
1. **Hydration & Diet**: Drink sufficient water frequently, even if not feeling thirsty. Consume oral rehydration solutions (ORS), homemade lemon water, buttermilk (chaas), and fresh coconut water. Avoid alcohol, tea, coffee, and carbonated soft drinks which dehydrate the body.
2. **Sun Protection**: Avoid going out in direct sun during peak afternoon hours (12:00 PM to 3:00 PM). 3. **Care for Animals and Infants**: Never leave children or pets inside parked vehicles. Ensure domestic and stray animals have access to shaded resting spots and clean drinking water bowls.
4. **First Aid for Heat Exhaustion / Heatstroke**: Move the affected individual to a cool, shaded area. Sponge body with cold water or apply ice packs to neck, armpits, and groin. Provide fluids if conscious. If the person is unconscious or vomiting, seek immediate emergency medical care.

## Which Systems Fail First
General planning knowledge (civic systems failure hierarchy during heatwaves):
1. **Immediate Stress (Peak Afternoon)**: Electrical distribution grids face severe transformer overheating and load tripping due to surging air conditioning and cooling demands; localized power outages cascade.
2. **Secondary Stress (Daily Cycle)**: Municipal water tanker supply and tail-end distribution struggle to meet heightened domestic consumption quotas.
3. **Tertiary Stress (Prolonged Event)**: Emergency hospital outpatient departments experience surge in dehydration, heat cramps, and heat exhaustion cases.

## Suggested Triage Criteria
Suggested triage criteria, not an official standard:
- **P1 (Critical / Emergency)**: Suspected heatstroke (unconsciousness, high body temperature, confusion, cessation of sweating, convulsions).
- **P2 (High Priority)**: Transformer blast or power outage affecting a hospital or large residential zone during severe heatwave alert.
- **P3 (Medium Priority)**: Complete drinking water shortage reported in an informal settlement or public shelter during peak summer.
- **P4 (Low Priority)**: General inquiries regarding heat advisory notifications or installation of shade nets at traffic junctions.
