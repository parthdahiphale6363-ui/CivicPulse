# Solid Waste Management Rules: Citizen Rights & Statutory Duties

## Regulatory Overview
The Solid Waste Management framework, notified under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, governs the collection, segregation, storage, transportation, processing, and disposal of municipal solid waste across urban local bodies.

## Statutory Duties of Waste Generators
Every household, commercial establishment, and institutional waste generator is mandated to adhere to the following statutory duties:

1. **Mandatory Source Segregation**:
   - **Wet Waste (Biodegradable)**: Kitchen refuse, vegetable and fruit peels, food scraps, fallen leaves, and organic matter meant for composting or bio-methanation.
   - **Dry Waste (Non-Biodegradable)**: Recyclable paper, plastic, cardboard, glass, metals, tetra packs, and rubber.
   - **Domestic Hazardous Waste**: Discarded paints, chemical containers, pesticide cans, CFLs/tube lights, discarded batteries, and expired medicines.
   - All streams must be kept separate and handed over directly to authorized municipal collectors or waste pickers.

2. **Sanitary Waste Packaging**:
   - Used sanitary pads, adult and baby diapers, bandages, and hygiene products must be securely wrapped in protective coverings before placing into designated dry waste collection streams to safeguard sanitation workers.

3. **Strict Prohibition on Littering and Open Burning**:
   - Burning leaves, plastics, domestic waste, or commercial packaging on streets, vacant plots, open drains, or riverbanks is strictly prohibited.
   - Littering or dumping mixed trash in public spaces or stormwater drains is prohibited.

4. **Bulk Waste Generator (BWG) Responsibilities**:
   - Large residential complexes, gated communities, commercial complexes, hotels, and educational institutions generating substantial waste volumes must maintain on-site processing facilities (such as composting or bio-methanation) for wet waste.
   - Segregated dry waste must be channelized to authorized recyclers or municipal collection networks.
